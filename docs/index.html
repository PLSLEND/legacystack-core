The strategy

Three moves, in the right order

Most people sell what grows to pay for what they need, and lose the growth. Old family fortunes were kept the other way round: hold the asset, borrow against it, hand it down. This protocol puts that discipline into a vault anyone can open, with rules that cannot be changed after the fact.

01

Grow

Hold an asset that compounds and keep holding it. Staked ether (wstETH) earns its own staking yield inside the vault, year after year, whether you borrow or not. The protocol never touches it except to protect itself.

Nobody sells your collateral for you unless it falls below the safety line you can read on the screen.

02

Borrow

Draw OCT, the protocol's loan unit, against your wstETH at a fixed 1.5% a year. No application, no credit file, no counterparty. You decide how much to draw; the protocol only enforces the 110% floor and a 0.5% mint fee.

OCT is minted when you borrow and burned when you repay. It is the unit of your loan, nothing more.

03

Pass it on

Name up to ten heirs and their shares. Every action you take with your key is a sign of life. After one year of silence and thirty days of grace, anyone may trigger the settlement: the vault pays its own debt at par and delivers what remains directly into your heirs' wallets.

No custodian, no paperwork, no death certificate. A clock, and a plan you can change at any time.

The mechanics

What stands behind every vault

Four mechanisms, all inside the protocol, all funded by the protocol's own fees. No treasury, no committee, no discretionary intervention.

Redemption Buffer

A reserve of wstETH that receives every fee and all interest. Redemptions are served from this reserve first, so your vault is touched only when the reserve is empty. Its size also sets how much the system may lend: $1,000,000 plus $100 for every $1 it holds.

Stability Pool

OCT deposited by participants absorbs liquidated debt and receives the liquidated collateral at a discount. It is the first line of defence against bad debt, and a position in it is part of your succession plan.

Price feed without keys

The price of wstETH is Chainlink's ETH/USD times Lido's exchange rate, nothing else. If either source misbehaves, the protocol goes into a reduce-only state: repaying, adding collateral, closing and liquidating at the last good price stay open; new risk waits. It heals by itself.

Immutable rules

Rate, fees, floor, ceiling formula, succession timing: all fixed at deployment. There is no owner, no upgrade path and no pause. This is what makes a promise to an heir in 2050 worth anything.

In plain words

  • The protocol holds no keys. Nobody can freeze it, upgrade it, change the rate or take the collateral. Nobody can help you either: write your heirs down.
  • Borrowing costs a 0.5% mint fee plus 1.5% a year. Redemptions carry a 0.75% minimum fee. Fees and interest feed the Redemption Buffer, which protects borrowers from redemptions and sets the lending limit.
  • If your collateral ratio falls under 110%, your vault can be liquidated by anyone. Keep a margin; the dashboard shows your liquidation price at all times.
  • LegacyStack is software. It is not a bank, an insurer or an investment firm. This page is a test deployment of unaudited code on a test network: it teaches the mechanics and is not an offer, advice or a promise.

Operator (optional)

An operator may add collateral and repay for you. If you allow it, it may also borrow and withdraw, always to your wallet, as long as the vault keeps the ratio floor you set. Operator actions never count as your sign of life.

Execute a plan (anyone)

When a wallet's plan is executable, anyone may call the settlement and is paid a small reward from the estate. If the system cannot settle the whole debt at par today, the call does what it can and may be repeated.
