A fixed-rate credit line against your assets. No middleman: only you and an immutable contract. You keep the asset that grows and keeps earning, you borrow against it instead of selling it, and the vault knows what to do when you are no longer there.

The strategy

One asset, working four times

Most people sell what grows to pay for what they need, and lose the growth. Family offices and private banks have always done the opposite: hold the asset, borrow against it, put the liquidity to work, hand the whole structure down. That playbook used to need a banker, a trust and a great deal of money. Here it needs a wallet. Each layer is money the same asset earns again.

01 · It grows

Hold

Staked ether (wstETH) earns its own staking yield inside the vault, year after year, whether you borrow or not. An asset that can grow, with real yield of its own.

Real yield: the rewards Ethereum pays the validators who run the network, from transaction fees and new issuance. The chain paying for its own security.

02 · It backs credit

Borrow

Draw OCT, the protocol's loan unit, against your wstETH at a fixed 1.5% a year. No application, no credit file, no counterparty. You decide how much to draw; the protocol enforces one rule, the 110% minimum collateral ratio.

OCT is minted when you borrow and burned when you repay: an over-collateralised unit, redeemable for a dollar's worth of collateral less the redemption fee, with no issuer and no freeze function. Only you and the contract can mint or burn it.

03 · The liquidity works

Deploy

The OCT you drew is yours. Inside the protocol it can earn in the Stability Pool, provide OCT/USDe liquidity, or buy more wstETH for the vault and raise your ratio. Every position inside the protocol stays inside your succession plan.

Or take it out. It is a token in your wallet like any other; use it wherever you judge best, further down the risk curve if you like. The vault does not care where it goes, it only watches the ratio. What leaves the protocol leaves the plan.

04 · It passes on

Pass it on

Name up to ten heirs and their shares. Once a year, press one button: that is your proof of life, and nothing else counts. After one year of silence and thirty days of grace, anyone may trigger the settlement: the vault's debt is paid from its own OCT, then from the reserve at its offer price, then by the Stability Pool at 2% over par, and what remains goes directly into your heirs' wallets.

No custodian, no paperwork, no death certificate. A clock, and a plan you can change at any time. The vault, your Stability Pool deposit, your reserve deposit and the liquidity you provide through the protocol all pass with it.

The mechanics

What stands behind every vault

Four mechanisms, all inside the protocol, all funded by the protocol's own fees. No treasury, no committee, no discretionary intervention, and no one between you and the contract: you decide, you act, the chain records it. Housekeeping is done by whoever presses the button and is paid for it.

Redemption Reserve

A reserve of wstETH that receives every fee and all interest. Redemptions are served from this reserve first, so your vault is touched only when the reserve is empty. Its size also sets how much the system may lend: $1,000,000 plus $100 for every $1 it holds.

Stability Pool

OCT deposited by participants absorbs liquidated debt and receives the liquidated collateral at a discount; when it settles an estate it is paid 2% over par. It is the first line of defence against bad debt, and a position in it is part of your succession plan.

Price feed without keys

ETH/USD from two independent oracle networks, Chainlink and Chronicle, times Lido's own exchange rate. Nothing else sets the price of wstETH, and nobody can switch the feed. Should the sources ever disagree or fall silent, new borrowing simply waits at the last good price while repaying, adding collateral and closing stay open, and lending resumes by itself when they agree again.

Immutable rules

Rate, fees, floor, ceiling formula, succession timing: all fixed at deployment. There is no owner, no upgrade path and no pause. This is what makes a promise to an heir in 2050 worth anything.

In plain words

  • There is no middleman. The protocol holds no keys: nobody can freeze it, upgrade it, change the rate or take the collateral. It is you, the contract and the blockchain. Nobody can help you either: write your heirs down.
  • Borrowing costs a 0.5% mint fee plus 1.5% a year. Redemptions carry a 0.75% minimum fee. Fees and interest feed the Redemption Reserve, which protects borrowers from redemptions and sets the lending limit.
  • If your collateral ratio falls under 110%, your vault can be liquidated by anyone. Keep a margin; the dashboard shows your liquidation price at all times.
  • Where the protocol pays for collateral, in new loans and the reserve's offer, it never values wstETH above its market price. If every price source were dead for thirty days, the deployment would stop lending for good and still settle every estate.
  • LegacyStack is software. It is not a bank, an insurer or an investment firm. This page is a test deployment of unaudited code on a test network: it teaches the mechanics and is not an offer, advice or a promise.

Help another vault

Anyone may add collateral to any vault, from their own wallet: a family member can make a vault safer during a crash while its owner is unreachable. It is the only thing a third party can do to a vault, it works even when the oracle is on hold, and it never counts as the owner's proof of life.

Keeper desk

The protocol has no staff. Whoever presses a button does that piece of housekeeping and keeps what the protocol pays for it: 200 OCT plus 0.5% of the collateral for every vault liquidated, up to 0.02 wstETH for every estate settled, 1% of the OCT (at most 5) for every auto-repay that is due. Three buttons, three jobs, so the urgent one never pays for the others.

Redemption Reserve testnet

The reserve

Every fee and all interest end up here. Redemptions are served from the reserve first, so vaults are touched only when it is empty. The reserve also sets the lending limit. What it holds above its target flows out through a waterfall: half of the band up to twice the target and everything above it, at most 5% a day, split between the burn auction and protocol-owned liquidity.

Reserve deposit

Lend the reserve your wstETH and keep it. A deposit serves redemptions like the rest of the pot, stays yours, is locked for 180 days from your last deposit, and can be withdrawn afterwards whenever the reserve stays at its target without it. In return you earn what the reserve earns for serving the market, the redemption fees and the standing offer's premium: depositors receive their share of the pot and never less than half, set aside in wstETH and claimable at any time, plus STACK for every day served while the rewards contract holds any. The deposit is part of your succession plan. What you risk is time, and holding OCT instead of wstETH if a redemption wave converts the pot before you leave.

Burn auction

Half of what the waterfall releases is sold here for STACK, lot by lot, and the STACK is burned. The price starts high and falls over 48 hours to a tenth of its start, then holds until someone buys; the next lot starts at twice the last clearing price. No pool, no router, no keys: two tokens and a clock, so it works in any decade. The other half goes to the protocol-owned-liquidity keeper, which turns it into OCT through the standing offer and places it one-sided in the market pool when OCT trades at or above par.
